        About the paints and your data

        Primers, varnishes, mediums, washes, shades, glazes, inks, transparents, dry paints, fluorescents, effect paints and plain black or white are left out. Metallic paints only come up in the metallic slot, never in the four colour slots. One-coat ranges leave out their metallics, mediums, glow effects and plain black or white. Swatches for one-coat paints show the colour over a white undercoat, roughly. Vallejo True Metallic Metal Shade and airbrush pots are left out, so only the Light and Base pots are in.

        Swatches marked ≈ are estimates, including all of Tone Pro, Vallejo True Metallic Metal and Darkstar, since official colour values aren't published. Swatches for the other ranges come from community colour data and are close, not exact.

        The John Blanche Masterclass Vol. 1–4 paints and the Warpaints Historical paints have their own switches (Army Painter Fanatic: John Blanche and Army Painter Fanatic: Historical), so you can leave them out if you don't own them. Army Painter's washes, effects (Grim Rust, Rust, Mud Spatter) and relabelled duplicates are left out, and the metallics are in the Fanatic Metallics ranges.

        Your saved default, projects, photos and badges are stored on this phone only. The app collects no personal data.

        Not affiliated with or endorsed by Games Workshop, The Army Painter, AK Interactive, Monument Hobbies, Privateer Press / Steamforged Games, Vallejo, Squidmar Miniatures, Scale75, Two Thin Coats or Darkstar. Paint names are used only to describe the paints. Colour data partly from the miniature-paints project by Rick Fleuren (MIT licence).

        Colour schemes use each swatch’s position on the colour wheel, so they’re only as accurate as the swatches. Estimated (≈) swatches can sit a little off their real hue.

        Colour families are worked out from each swatch, so a paint that sits between two colours (a teal-blue, a red-brown) can land in either neighbour.

        How to

        Everything you need to get the most out of your challenges.

        How a challenge works

        1. Roll. You get four colours and a metallic. Paint your mini with only those, plus black and white.
        2. Lock a pot you like before rolling again to keep it. Swap changes just that one pot.
        3. No two pots share a colour family, so you never get two blues or two reds.
        4. Share posts an image of your challenge to Instagram, Facebook, Discord and more. Add the mini's name first to include it.
        5. Save as project adds the challenge to the Projects tab, where you can track it to the finish.

        Projects, streaks and badges

        1. Name your mini on the Roll screen and tap Save as project. It appears on the Projects tab as Rolled.
        2. Tap a project to move it to Painting, then Finished. The dates fill in for you, and you can change them.
        3. Add a before and an after photo, then tap Share before & after for a ready-made post with the logo and #MyMiniPaintingChallenge. Pick Tall for stories or Square for Instagram posts, so nothing gets cropped.
        4. Your streak counts the months in a row in which you finished at least one mini. Finish one before the month ends to keep it going.
        5. Badges unlock as you finish minis, keep your streak, try every scheme and palette, share your work and more. Tap a badge to see how to earn it.

        Seasonal challenge

        Every season a new challenge appears at the top of the Projects tab: four paints and a metallic, set by @myminipaintingchallenge. You have the whole season to paint it: spring (March to May), summer (June to August), autumn (September to November) and winter (December to February). Next to each paint you'll see your closest paint from the ranges you've switched on, or "You have this". Tap Start this challenge to add it to your projects, and finish it to earn the seasonal badges. Say yes to the reminder and your phone will let you know when each season starts.

        Event badges

        Special events have their own exclusive badges. At an in-person event, look for the event code on the table and type it into Got an event code? under your badges on the Projects tab. Online events unlock by themselves when you finish a mini (or roll a challenge) during the event. Event badges appear in their own row, only once you've earned them, so they show you were there.

        Release day challenges

        When a new paint range comes out, a release day challenge appears on the Projects tab and runs for 4 weeks. Tap Roll my challenge: you get 2 paints from the new release and 2 from your own ranges, plus a metallic, and they still follow your colour scheme and palette. Lock and swap as usual (a swapped new-release pot stays in the new release), then save it as a project. Only two new pots to buy! Finish it to earn the Fresh Pots badge. With reminders on, your phone lets you know on release day.

        Set up your defaults

        1. Open My paints.
        2. Pick a colour scheme, or Mix it up to get a different one each roll.
        3. Pick a paint type: Acrylic, One coat (Contrast-style paints) or a Mixture.
        4. Switch on only the paint ranges you own. "All on" and "Only one" are quick shortcuts.
        5. Tap Save as my default. The app opens with these settings every time. Changed your mind? Undo changes goes back to your saved default.

        Paint type and palette can also be changed on the Roll screen for a single session.

        The colour wheel

        Colour schemes use the colour wheel to pick paints that work together. Colours opposite each other contrast strongly and make each other pop. Colours side by side blend calmly. Each pot shows its job in the scheme:

        • Main – the colour that leads. Use it on the biggest areas.
        • Complement / Split / Second – the contrast colours. Use them for details and spot colours.
        • Neighbour – a calmer colour next to the main one, good for cloth or secondary armour.
        • Neutral – a grey, brown or bone that ties everything together: leather, bases, weapons.

        Opposite colours are opposite in temperature too. A warm red (leaning towards orange) pairs with a cool teal (leaning towards blue), and a cool red (leaning towards pink) pairs with a warm teal (leaning towards green). The app always picks contrast colours that lean the other way from the colour they sit opposite.

        Colour schemes

        Analogous

        A main colour and its neighbours either side on the wheel, plus a neutral. Colours that sit side by side always get on, so the result is calm and natural, like a sunset (red, orange, yellow) or a forest (yellow, green, teal).

        Tip: there's little contrast between the colours, so get it from light and dark instead: deep shadows and bright highlights.

        Complementary

        A main colour, its opposite on the wheel, a neighbour of the main colour and a neutral. The classic high-contrast look, like red armour with green details.

        Tip: let the main colour cover most of the model and keep its opposite for small spot colours.

        Golden angle

        A main colour, then two more colours, each 137.5° further round the wheel, plus a neutral. 137.5° is the golden angle, the same spacing sunflowers use for their seeds. The colours are always well spread but never exactly opposite, so it feels fresh without clashing.

        Tip: let the main colour lead and use the two golden colours for cloth, gems and details.

        Mother colour

        A bold triad (three colours evenly spaced round the wheel) plus a neutral, where one of the three is the mother colour (the big dot). Mix a little of it into every other paint as you go, or glaze it thinly over the whole mini at the end. Every colour then shares a bit of the mother, so even loud colours look like they belong together.

        Tip: start with about one part mother to five parts paint, and add more for a stronger mood.

        Random

        No colour theory: four paints from four different colour families. The wildest challenge.

        Split complementary

        A main colour plus the two colours either side of its opposite, with a neutral. Strong contrast, but easier to balance than a straight complementary.

        Tip: use one of the split colours for gems and lenses and the other for cloth.

        Tetrad

        Two pairs of opposite colours. Bold and colourful, so it suits busy or cartoon-style minis.

        Tip: pick one colour to lead and use the other three sparingly, or it can look noisy.

        Mix it up

        Each roll picks one of the schemes above at random, so you never know what's coming.

        Palettes

        Palettes on the Roll screen set the mood by narrowing which paints can come up. They work alongside any colour scheme. With Cold or Warm and a colour scheme, the main colours take the palette’s temperature and the contrast colours take the other one: warm colours with cool accents, or cold colours with a warm glow.

        • Bright – strong, saturated, neon-style colours.
        • Cold – the cooler version of every colour: greyed, muted shades and blue-grey neutrals, with steel for the metallic.
        • Grimdark – dark, muted and dirty: earth tones, gunmetal greys, bruised reds and sickly greens.
        • Historical – colours real armies and people wore: earth pigments, madder reds, indigo blues, drab greens, leather and bone, with plain steel, brass, bronze or gold for the metallic. Great for historical wargames, and for grounded fantasy too.
        • Warm – the warmer version of every colour: rich, saturated shades and brown-tinted neutrals, with gold or copper for the metallic. For example, Thousand Sons Blue is a warm blue and The Fang is a cold one.

        Good to know

        • Swatches are a guide to each paint's colour. Ones marked ≈ are estimates, so check the real pot.
        • One-coat paints look different over light and dark undercoats. Try a white or zenithal undercoat for the brightest results.
        • The metallic is always extra and isn't part of the colour scheme.
